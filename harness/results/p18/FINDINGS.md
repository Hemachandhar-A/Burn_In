# P1.8 findings - read before building the differentiation slide

Hand-written interpretation by P1 of the generated tables in this folder (`comparison.md` and the CSVs,
regenerated by `python -m harness.comparison`). Every number below is copied from those files. Seed 2026, the
same five held-out families as the P1.7 bake-off: 9,779 parts, 521 defective. Everything is judged on the full
0h-168h Complete-lot series, at the committed thresholds (max; REVIEW 0.955628, REJECT 0.964286).

## Summary: the harness does not support "Module A beats the industry baselines"

| method | share flagged | recall | precision | cost / part (FN:FP 10:1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| static datasheet limits | 0.2% | 4.0% | 100% | 0.511 |
| fixed delta limits | 24.0% | **100%** | 22.2% | **0.186** |
| static PAT | 6.8% | 44.7% | 35.0% | 0.339 |
| dynamic PAT (DPAT) | 3.2% | 59.1% | **97.2%** | 0.219 |
| Module A, REVIEW | 24.3% | 84.1% | 18.4% | 0.283 |
| Module A, REJECT (pre-cap) | 20.4% | 78.7% | 20.5% | 0.276 |

- **Where Module A wins:** its recall at REVIEW is well above static limits, static PAT and DPAT. Two checks
  confirm this is real, not just a function of flagging more parts:
  - it has the highest recall on every defect archetype except against fixed delta;
  - its per-part cost (10:1) is lower than static limits and static PAT.
- **Where it loses:**
  - Fixed delta limits catch every defect at a slightly *lower* share flagged (24.0% vs 24.3%) and a lower cost.
  - DPAT has a lower cost than Module A at 97% precision.
  - Given the *same number of flags* as each baseline (`matched_flag_budget.csv`), Module A has lower recall
    than **every** baseline. At DPAT's 317 flags it gets 30.7% recall against DPAT's 59.1%.
- **Golden worked example** (`golden_example.csv`):
  - The static limit misses it (0.9 of the 50 uA limit), which is the problem statement's point.
  - Fixed delta misses it too (the part never drifts).
  - Module A flags it at REJECT, but **DPAT also flags it** (2.6x its limit).
  - So the worked example separates Module A from datasheet and delta limits, not from DPAT.

## Why, as far as P1 can tell (diagnosis, not a fix - these sit in other owners' directories or decisions)

1. **Module A's score is a rank, not a magnitude.** E2 step 5 percentile-normalizes each detector within the lot, and the
   part's severity is the max of 12 such ranks (3 parameters x 4 detectors). So every lot's top ~20% score near
   1.0 whether or not anything in it is abnormal. The score cannot tell "6 robust sigma out" from "top 5% of a
   normal lot". DPAT's limit is a magnitude (6 robust sigma), which is exactly why it is so precise. This is the
   design as specified (E2 step 5, context.md 6.1), implemented faithfully by module_a. Changing it is a Lead/P3
   decision.
2. **Fixed delta is favoured by construction here.** P1.5 set its allowances from the generator's own
   healthy-drift spread (`harness/industry_baselines.py`, disclosed there). Also, every generator defect archetype is a
   *drift* trajectory. A drift-allowance baseline tuned on the healthy drift distribution therefore catches all
   of them. A real datasheet delta limit is written without knowing the lots, so real-world fixed-delta
   recall would be lower. How much lower is not measurable from synthetic data.
3. Module A's Isolation Forest and ECOD see only 0h/24h values (CONTRACT_CHANGES.md, the open "IF/ECOD feature scope" entry), and
   its robust z is on levels, not deltas. So a part that drifts late from a normal start is caught mainly by the
   168h z-score.

## What the slide can honestly claim today

- Module A catches the worked example that datasheet limits miss. So does DPAT, which the slide should
  acknowledge.
- At its operating point Module A reaches 84% recall vs 59% (DPAT), 45% (static PAT) and 4% (datasheet), but at a much
  higher flag rate. That is a recall-for-review-load trade, not a free win.
- Module B beats every physics baseline (persistence, linear, power law) on MAE in 15 of 15 cells at the 24h
  horizon and 10 of 15 at 96h (`module_b_summary.csv`). Interval coverage is below the 90% target on the
  `different_noise_regime` family (minimum 44%), so the coverage claim needs that caveat.
- Differentiation in *method* (benchmarking against named industry baselines at all, and disclosing results
  like these) is real (context.md 2.3). Claiming outperformance of fixed delta or DPAT is not supported.
